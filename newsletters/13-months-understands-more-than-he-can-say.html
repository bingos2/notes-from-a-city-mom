Your 13-Month-Old Understands More Than He Can Say

How to build communication before the words really come.

Around the first birthday, communication is already much bigger than spoken vocabulary. Your child may respond to familiar words, connect phrases with daily routines, point or gesture toward what he wants, babble back and forth with you, and begin understanding very simple communication paired with gestures.

That means you don’t need to wait for a bigger vocabulary to start teaching.

Try this this week

  1. Keep sentences short. Try “Shoes on.” “Bath time.” “Give it to me.”
  2. Repeat the same words inside the same routines. Predictable repetition helps words become connected with actions.
  3. Add a gesture. Say “come here” while opening your arms, or “give it to me” while holding out your hand.
  4. Treat his attempts as communication. If he points, babbles, looks, or makes a sound to get your attention, respond.
  5. Pause. Give him a moment to answer with a look, gesture, sound, movement, or word.

Five phrases to repeat all week

“Give it to me.”
“All done.”
“Let’s go.”
“More?”
“Where’s your ___?”

When to bring up a concern

Development varies, but talk with your pediatrician if you are worried about how your child hears, responds to voices or sounds, interacts with you, uses gestures or vocalizations, or if he loses a skill he previously had.

Source note: American Academy of Pediatrics / HealthyChildren.org and CDC developmental milestone guidance.
