13 Months · Communication

Your 13-month-old may understand more than he can say.

Around the first birthday, communication is already much bigger than spoken vocabulary.

Your child may connect familiar words with routines, point or gesture toward what he wants, babble back and forth, and use eye contact or sounds to keep an interaction going.

The useful shift: Don’t wait for perfect speech to start teaching. Treat looks, gestures, sounds, and movement as part of the conversation.

Try these four things

  1. Use short phrases. “Shoes on.” “Bath time.” “Give it to me.”
  2. Repeat key words during routines. Predictability helps words connect with actions.
  3. Add a gesture. Hold out your hand, point, open your arms, or model the movement.
  4. Pause and answer. When he points, babbles, looks, or makes a sound, respond like it matters.

Why this matters

You’re not only teaching vocabulary. You’re teaching the back-and-forth pattern of communication: I signal, you respond, then I respond again.

When to bring up a concern

Development varies. Talk with your pediatrician if you’re concerned about hearing, response to voices or sounds, interaction, gestures or vocalizations, or if your child loses a skill previously learned.

Expert-source note: American Academy of Pediatrics / HealthyChildren.org guidance on early language development and CDC developmental milestone resources for 1 year and 15 months. Thirteen months is treated here as a stage between those milestone checkpoints.
